We assemble flat-pack furniture for a living, and most of what fails was not a bad product. It was assembled out of order, installed against the wrong wall, or fastened with the wrong thing.

## Assembly order is not the order in the instructions

The instruction sheet is written for one person working alone on a clean floor with no time limit. That is not how it goes.

The mistakes we see most:

- **Inserting the cam bolts backward.** The cam lock has a head and a slot. The bolt goes in the panel, the lock goes in the mating panel, and the arrow on the lock points toward the edge. Backward, the joint will hold until it gets loaded, then separates.
- **Tightening everything as you go.** Loose-fit the whole frame, square it, then snug. A cabinet tightened panel by panel pulls out of square and the doors never sit right.
- **Attaching the back panel before squaring.** The thin back panel is the structural member that keeps a bookcase square. Fit it last, and check the diagonals with a tape before you fasten it. Equal diagonals means square.
- **Skipping the anti-tip hardware.** This is not optional. See below.

## Drywall anchors: the wrong one is worse than none

An anchor's job is to transfer load from the screw into the substrate. The common mistake is using the anchor that came in the box for a wall it does not suit.

| Anchor | Best for | Load (each, in 1/2 inch drywall) | Do not use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plastic ribbed | Light loads | 20–35 lb | Anything that will be loaded or leaned on |
| Self-drilling (zinc) | Light to moderate | 30–50 lb | Ceilings, or near a stud you could use instead |
| Toggle bolt | Heavy loads on drywall | 50–80 lb | Thin panels, or where the hole already blew out |
| Snap toggle | Heavy loads, removable | 70–100 lb | Very shallow wall cavities |
| Lag bolt into a stud | Anything heavy | 100+ lb | A wall where you have not confirmed the stud |

A TV mount, a bookshelf over 5 feet, a wall cabinet, or anything a child might hang from goes into a stud or gets a snap toggle. A plastic ribbed anchor holding a loaded bookshelf is a bookshelf on the floor.

To find a stud reliably: a magnetic stud finder finds the drywall screws, which is more trustworthy than a capacitance finder in a house with metal lath. Confirm with a small nail where it will be hidden.

## Anti-tip is the whole point

Every tall dresser sold in the US since 2020 is required to come with an anti-tip kit, and most are never installed. A dresser that tips is the leading furniture-related injury mechanism for children in this country, and it does not require climbing. An open drawer with weight in it is enough.

The kit is two brackets and two screws. It goes into a stud, or into drywall with a snap toggle if there is no stud, and it takes ten minutes. If a dresser was assembled and the kit went in the trash, we install one for $65–$95.

## Lifting and damage

Two things protect the floor and the furniture:

- **A moving blanket or cardboard under the work area.** Assembly on a hard floor puts pressure on every edge and corner you set down.
- **Two people for anything over four feet.** Not because of the weight, because of the moment. A 60-inch bookcase handled by one person twists, and a twisted back panel never comes square.

## The questions to ask before furniture goes in a room

1. **Is the piece taller than it is deep?** It needs an anti-tip anchor.
2. **Is there a stud where it goes?** Find it before you place the piece.
3. **Is the door swing clear** with the piece in place, and does a drawer pull without hitting the wall?
4. **Will the piece sit on carpet?** A bookcase on thick carpet settles unevenly; adjustable feet or a hard pad under it.
5. **Is there someone in the house who will open every drawer at once?** Then anchor it. That is the whole conversation.
