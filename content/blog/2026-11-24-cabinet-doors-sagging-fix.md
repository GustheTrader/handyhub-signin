A cabinet door that sags is almost never a broken door. It is a hinge that came loose, a hinge that is adjusted wrong, a screw that has lost its bite, or a box that was installed out of square. Each has a different fix and only one of them costs money.

## Step one: find the cause in ninety seconds

Open the door and look at the **hinge plate where it meets the cabinet box.** There are four outcomes:

**The screws turn freely and do not tighten.** The screw holes have stripped the particleboard. This is the most common case, and it is a $4 fix. It is not a new door.

**The screws are tight but the door hangs low.** The hinge is adjustable. A European-style concealed hinge has three adjustments: side-to-side (the mounting plate screws), up-down, and in-out via the cam screws on the hinge cup. Ten minutes with a Phillips and a Torx or Pozi driver.

**One hinge is tight and one is loose.** The door is twisting. Adjust the loose one first, then re-check.

**The door is tight at one corner and open at the other diagonal.** The cabinet box is out of square or the door is warped. Run a straightedge along the door edge against a light. A gap that is even means it is adjustable. A door that rocks on a flat surface is warped and needs replacing.

## The stripped screw hole

The reason cabinet hinges pull out is that they are screwed into 5/8-inch particleboard, and a hinge carries the entire door load through two screws per hinge. Over ten years of opening a door 20 times a day, that is roughly 70,000 cycles on a fiberboard edge.

**The fix, cheapest to best:**

1. **Toothpicks and wood glue.** Fill the hole with glue, pack in toothpicks or a golf tee coated in glue, let it cure, and re-drive the screw into fresh material. Costs nothing and holds for years on a light door.
2. **Move the hinge.** A concealed hinge plate mounts with two screws on a 32mm system. You can often shift the plate up or down 5mm into fresh board with the same screw pattern and no new holes visible. This is the fix we use most.
3. **A dowel plug.** Drill the stripped hole to 3/8 inch, glue in a hardwood dowel, cut flush, and re-drive. Stronger than toothpicks and invisible once the door is closed.
4. **A hinge repair plate.** A $3 steel plate that wraps the cabinet edge and gives the screw a new surface. Visible when the door is open, so use it where the cabinet is not on display.
5. **Replace the cabinet side.** Rarely necessary. If the box itself is splitting, a fresh 3/4-inch panel is cheaper than a new cabinet.

**What not to do:** oversized screws. A #10 in a #8 hole splits the board and turns a $4 fix into a $200 door.

## Drawers that rub or stick

A drawer that rubs at the front face has three possible causes:

- **The slides are loose** at the cabinet side. Re-drive into fresh material, same as the hinge.
- **The drawer box is out of square.** Pull the drawer all the way out and measure the two diagonals. Equal means square.
- **The slides are worn.** Ball-bearing slides have a life, roughly 50,000 extended cycles. A $20–$40 pair replaces a side-mount; soft-close undermounts run $45–$90 a pair and are worth it on the drawer you use most.

A drawer that falls out has a broken **drawer box joint**. A box that has pulled apart at the back goes back together with glue and a clamp, not with a replacement.

## Doors: the ones worth actually replacing

If a **door is warped**, replace it. A warped door cannot be adjusted true. Custom cabinet doors in a matching style run $85–$200 each depending on material and finish, and hinges and pulls transfer.

If the door is **swollen from moisture at the bottom** — common near a dishwasher or under a sink — the door is done. Fix the moisture source first. A new door over a continuing leak is a repeat customer.

If the door is **merely dated**, refacing costs 60–70 percent less than new cabinets and keeps the boxes, which are usually fine.

## The check nobody does

Open every cabinet door fully and push **down** on the outside corner with light pressure. A door that gives more than a millimeter at the hinge has a loosening screw, and it is months away from pulling out. Catching it there costs $25. Catching it after the door strips the board costs $45 and leaves a repaired hole.
